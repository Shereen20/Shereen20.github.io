Applied Scientist Intern, Amazon (Central Machine Learning Team)
Jan – Jun '26 · Bangalore · Manager: Kaveri Sivaramakrishnan · Mentors: Anshuman Mourya, Rajdeep Mukerjee

A single profile rarely captures everything a person cares about. At Amazon I developed ASPEX, a framework that builds personas around the distinct aspects of a user's preferences. It induces a hierarchical taxonomy of aspects, assigns users to them softly, and refines persona prompts with OPRO. I evaluated it on eight personalization tasks across Amazon data and three public datasets, covering more than 300K users, with persona construction kept strictly separate in time from evaluation.

ASPEX outperformed contemporary persona-generation baselines, improving PR-AUC by a relative 24.2% and ROC-AUC by 15.8% on engagement prediction, and raising the recommendation follow-up rate by 18.9%. I also applied the same aspect-grounded idea as a verification step for production LLM inference on AWS Bedrock with Claude models, cutting end-to-end latency by 20.3%. The paper is submitted to the EACL 2027 Industry Track, and the project earned second place in the internal hackathon for applied scientist interns.

Student Researcher, Google DeepMind (Frontier AI, AQUA team)
Jun – Dec '25 · Bangalore · Manager: Sagar Gubbi · Skip-manager: Aravindan Raghuveer · Mentors: Deepanway Ghosal, Hiroki Furuta

I worked on a tool-use agent built on Gemini 3.5 Pro, and on a question that kept surfacing: how well do frontier agents hold up when the world stops looking like their benchmarks? I built VISTA-Bench, an adversarial evaluation framework that generates diverse test environments through a symmetric adversarial protocol. It spans more than 300 synthetic mobile environments, tracks compositional generalization, trajectory consistency and recovery from multi-step errors, and cut evaluation overhead by 35%.

The results were sobering: agents scoring above 90% on standard benchmarks lost up to 42% of their success rate under distribution shift. We also found a surprise, that agents generalized 22% better out of distribution in low-resource languages than in high-resource ones.
