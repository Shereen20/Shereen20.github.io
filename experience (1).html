Researcher, Microsoft Research
Oct '26 – present · Bangalore · Manager: Gopal Srinivasan

I work on evaluating coding agents, and on whether passing the provided tests is real evidence that code is correct. I built a state-coverage tool for C#/.NET on Roslyn. It parses a repository, builds a call graph that includes interface and virtual dispatch, and extracts every externally callable entry point along with its parameters, fields, static state, return values and external side effects. An agentic generator–checker–reviewer loop across two LLM families then abstracts each variable's domain into bounded value classes with executable C# predicates, and derives per-function path diagrams and class-level state machines. Redesigning the prompt around explicit breadth and depth budgets shrank the generated models about 4×.

Runtime instrumentation through Roslyn source rewriting measures value-class, path, state and transition coverage during dotnet test. Fully passing suites reached only 35–40% of input classes, against 83–90% for extended suites, and the tool surfaced real defects such as missing rollback and uncaught exceptions. I also evaluated test adequacy on SWE-bench Verified, EvalPlus and kGym, which shows that passing the provided tests overstates correctness. LLM-generated mocks raised state coverage from 41% to 99% once missed states were fed back. This work, GHOST, is co-authored with G. Srinivasa, S. Mohan, A. Rastogi and A. Prasad, and is under review at ISSTA 2027.

Applied Scientist Intern, Amazon (Central Machine Learning Team)
Jan – Jun '26 · Bangalore · Manager: Kaveri Sivaramakrishnan · Mentors: Anshuman Mourya, Rajdeep Mukerjee

A single profile rarely captures everything a person cares about. At Amazon I developed ASPEX, a framework that builds personas around the distinct aspects of a user's preferences. It induces a hierarchical taxonomy of aspects, assigns users to them softly, and refines persona prompts with OPRO. I evaluated it on eight personalization tasks across Amazon data and three public datasets, covering more than 300K users, with persona construction kept strictly separate in time from evaluation.

ASPEX outperformed contemporary persona-generation baselines, improving PR-AUC by a relative 24.2% and ROC-AUC by 15.8% on engagement prediction, and raising the recommendation follow-up rate by 18.9%. I also applied the same aspect-grounded idea as a verification step for production LLM inference on AWS Bedrock with Claude models, cutting end-to-end latency by 20.3%. The paper, co-authored with A. Mourya, R. Mukerjee, V. Puranik and S. Kaveri, is submitted to the EACL 2027 Industry Track, and the project earned second place in the internal hackathon for applied scientist interns.

Student Researcher, Google DeepMind (Frontier AI, AQUA team)
Jun – Dec '25 · Bangalore · Manager: Sagar Gubbi · Skip-manager: Aravindan Raghuveer · Mentors: Deepanway Ghosal, Hiroki Furuta

I worked on a tool-use agent built on Gemini 3.5 Pro, and on a question that kept surfacing: how well do frontier agents hold up when the world stops looking like their benchmarks? I built VISTA-Bench, an adversarial evaluation framework that generates diverse test environments through a symmetric adversarial protocol. It spans more than 300 synthetic mobile environments, tracks compositional generalization, trajectory consistency and recovery from multi-step errors, and cut evaluation overhead by 35%.

The results were sobering: agents scoring above 90% on standard benchmarks lost up to 42% of their success rate under distribution shift. We also found a surprise, that agents generalized 22% better out of distribution in low-resource languages than in high-resource ones.

Research Assistant, Harvard Medical School (Marks Lab)
From Nov '26 · Remote · Advisor: Prof. Debora Marks

I benchmark general-purpose language models against specialist protein-variant predictors on shared candidate sets, comparing predicted scores with measured mutation effects. Research Collaborator, University of Calgary (DENOS Lab)
Oct '26 – present · Remote · Advisor: Prof. Steve Drew · Collaborator: Jiajun Wu

I work on EDSim, an LLM-agent emergency department simulator in which GPT-4o-mini-driven patient, triage nurse, bedside nurse and physician agents run the full care journey. The paper, co-authored with J. Wu, Prof. S. Drew and the DENOS Lab team, is under review at the ICML 2027 main track. I check simulated wait times against historical data from a Level-1 trauma centre across five CTAS acuity levels, and the baseline outputs match the historical wait-time distributions.

The simulator also lets us ask what-if questions. In surge experiments over 326 simulated patient visits, a 60% increase in arrival rate raised mean length of stay by 75.4%, from 552 to 968 minutes. In a replica of a 79-bed, 15-physician department, arrival-to-assessment times rose 74.7% and post-disposition boarding rose 100.7% under stress, which shows where the bottlenecks form.
